# Reconstructing the Kerala channels

What the geomorphological literature actually supports, after three surface
methods failed and the borehole evidence turned out to say something other than
what I expected.

## The buried sands are old shorelines, not old rivers

Eight of the seventeen boreholes in Maya et al. 2017 contain a sand body sealed
under finer sediment, which is the classic signature of a buried channel. They
are not channels. The paper's own interpretation is explicit:

> "The medium to fine grained, well sorted quartzose sand lying directly above
> the greenish grey clay in the Komallur borehole core is nothing but the beach/
> littoral sediments formed under regressive phase."

> "The sediments in the inland wetlands on the east give indications of
> transgressive-regressive sequence with marine sediments at the base followed
> upwards by transitional and fresh water environments."

The fauna agree. Marine indicators, pelecypods, gastropods, dinoflagellates and
foraminiferal linings, appear in eight of the seventeen cores including every
one of the thick buried sands. These are transgressive and regressive beach
deposits recording the sea advancing and retreating, not rivers.

**So the borehole route does not deliver palaeochannels either.** That is four
methods now negative for Kerala: optical, radar, terrain, and subsurface
lithology. Recorded in `data/raw/kerala_boreholes.csv`.

## But two things in the same paper do bear on the question

**The inland wetland chain is drowned river valley.** The paper states it
directly:

> "The chain of wetlands and water bodies that are seen in the eastern periphery
> of the coastal lands indicates the remnants of the upper drainage channels of
> the previously existed coastal plain rivers of Late Pleistocene age that are
> later broadened due to coastal erosion under the transgressive phase."

That is a palaeochannel statement about a feature we can already map, because
the wetland chain is in the JRC water layer. It does not date to the first
century, but it constrains where drainage ran.

**Two beach-ridge sets bound what land existed.** The paper distinguishes a
NNE-SSW trending inland set marking the Late Pleistocene transgression from a
coast-parallel younger set marking the Mid Holocene one:

> "The present beach parallel ridges in the younger coastal plain indicate the
> limit of the Mid Holocene shoreline."

Everything seaward of the inland edge of the younger ridge set accreted after
the mid-Holocene. That is a mappable line, and it is the nearest dated shoreline
constraint to antiquity that exists for this coast. Any candidate sitting
seaward of it needs its ground shown to have existed by AD 50.

## What this means for the thesis

The honest position on Kerala is that no method recovers a first-century channel
network, and four have been tried. What the geomorphology gives instead is a
set of constraints: which ground is too young, where drainage formerly ran, and
how far the sea reached at two dated moments.

That is a prior on the landscape, not a map of it. The distance analysis has to
be run over the modern and 1857 water networks with that uncertainty carried,
rather than over a reconstructed first-century one that cannot be built.

## Still to read

Narayana, Priju and Chakrabarti (2001) on a palaeo-delta at the mouth of the
Periyar is the one remaining lead, and it addresses Muziris directly. Maya et
al. 2022 for the Kuttanad boreholes specifically, and Alappat et al. 2015, whose
OSL dates may reach closer to the first century than the radiocarbon does.
