# Evidence and data sources

Four evidence streams. Candidates for a given port sit 25 to 150 km apart, so
resolution at that scale is the criterion throughout.

---

## 1. Geographic

The blocking stream. The Periplus gives the Malabar distances by river and by
sea, so no candidate can be measured against them until the channels are
reconstructed.

- **Sediment cores.** Five boreholes through lower Kuttanad at Nedumudi, Alappuzha, Kaniyamkulam, Thakazhi and Kidangara, plus Vembanad lagoon cores with Holocene mud dated 9700 to 3220 BP. The only evidence that is both dated and located at the candidates. Published in Maya et al. 2022 and the related NCESS papers; not yet acquired.
- **Sea-level curve.** Shaji et al. 2022 for Kerala, Weil et al. 2022 for the Gulf of Aqaba. The Red Sea curve needs the Aqaba transform uplift subtracted before it is usable at Aynuna.
- **Dated shorelines.** Alappat et al. 2015, OSL on palaeo-beach ridges, giving successive shoreline positions with dates.
- **Offshore cores.** GeoB12624 off the Rufiji mouth, open on PANGAEA, with pollen, XRF and grain size. A discharge record rather than a shoreline record.
- **Historic charts.** Admiralty 750 and 751 of 1857, their manuscript originals in TNA series ADM 352, and Survey of India sheet 58 C/NW surveyed 1911 to 1913. These predate the Kuttanad reclamation, the Thanneermukkom barrage and the Idukki dam, so they bound how much water has been removed. Not acquired.
- **Declassified imagery.** CORONA, 1960 to 1972, via USGS EarthExplorer and the CORONA Atlas. Shows relict channel traces before modern land use erased them. Not ordered.
- **Terrain.** FABDEM, the Copernicus DEM with canopy and buildings removed, checked against ICESat-2 ground returns. Necessary because a dead channel has one or two metres of relief and raw satellite elevation measures treetops.
- **River network and bathymetry.** HydroRIVERS, held. GEBCO, open and unused.
- **Wind.** ERA5 from the Copernicus climate data store, open; the voyage model is not built.

*Only the cores carry first-century dates. Everything else gives a pre-industrial baseline that bounds how much has since changed.*

## 2. Material

- **Survey effort.** *Indian Archaeology: A Review*, 1953-54 to 2013-14, 50 volumes parsed cleanly from 78 held, yielding 7,053 fieldwork entries. This is the only systematic record of where anyone has looked, which is what makes an absence of finds readable at all. Thirteen of twenty-two candidates have never been excavated, and across Kerala only four entries in the whole series mention material of Periplus date.
- **Excavation campaigns.** 27 campaigns hand-curated from site reports, covering all 23 candidates, recording seasons dug, whether virgin soil was reached, and whether the dig was explicitly searching for the port.
- **Coin hoards.** The Coin Hoards of the Roman Empire database, cleaned from 627 to 427 hoards and 7,778 coins by dropping out-of-region records, records without coordinates, and one hoard georeferenced about 260 km from its findspot. The result is a regional trade signal, not a locational one: 73 per cent of recorded coins lie more than 25 km inland, concentrated on the Palghat corridor, so hoards cannot point at a harbour. The category that would bear on harbour location is single and stray finds, which the database omits and which is catalogued in Turner 1989, not yet acquired.
- **Quantified finds.** 61 rows from 6 sites, with ware typology from Tomber 2008 so that "Roman pottery at X" in the older Indian reports can be compared across reports. Pattanam's own published import share disagrees with itself by a factor of five.
- **Harbour structures.** A wharf, a six-metre canoe and nine teak bollards at Pattanam, radiocarbon dated to the first century BC or AD. The only structural evidence of a port at any candidate.

*A positive find of the right date is close to decisive. An absence is almost always an absence of excavation.*

## 3. Textual

- **Periplus Maris Erythraei**, in Schoff 1912 and Casson 1989. 51 distance statements and the descriptions of each port.
- **Ptolemy, *Geography***, with McCrindle 1885 as the standard English commentary. 23 relevant entries.
- **Pliny, *Naturalis Historia*** book 6.
- **The Muziris papyrus**, edited by de Romanis.
- **Tamil Sangam poems**, held at second hand only.
- **Tabula Peutingeriana.**

*The distances are rounded day-counts, not measurements: 82 per cent of stadia figures are multiples of 100 and every value at or above 2,000 is an exact multiple of 1,000. They yield rankings, not fits.*

## 4. Later settlement

- Medieval and early modern port records, and place-name continuity, per candidate.
- Colonial survey maps, so far only through Gurukkal and Whittaker 2001.

*Every Kerala candidate is a documented later port with no first-century evidence. This stream is how the candidate list was assembled, so it is a source of bias in the hypothesis set rather than evidence for any member of it.*
