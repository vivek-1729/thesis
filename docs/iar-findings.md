# Indian Archaeology: A Review — what 59 years of fieldwork records say

## The corpus

78 volumes covering **59 distinct years, 1953–54 to 2013–14** (only 1990–91 and
2000–01 are missing), now held as searchable text in `library/iar-text/`.
IAR is the annual record of every excavation and exploration reported in India.
If a candidate site has been dug, it is here.

**How it was obtained.** archive.org's derived `_djvu.txt` files are unusable —
the Digital Library of India scanned these volumes with a Devanagari OCR model,
so the English came out as mojibake, and an early search over them wrongly
showed every Kerala candidate absent. But the **PDFs themselves carry a clean
English text layer**. No OCR was needed: download, extract, done. The ASI site
supplies 2001–2014 directly.

Search tool: `src/search_iar.py`. Hits: `data/processed/iar_hits.csv`.

Two false-positive classes are flagged rather than silently dropped. *Kollam* is
usually the **Kollam era**, a dating system used across the Tamil country, not
the port. *Pattanam* simply means "town", so 1983–84 and 1988–89 hits are Tondi
in Tamil Nadu and a village in Telangana.

## The result

**In 59 years of Indian excavation reporting, exactly one site in Kerala has
yielded Roman imported pottery: Pattanam.**

### Tyndis

| Candidate | What IAR records |
|---|---|
| Ponnani | Named in 9 volumes, but always as Ponnani **taluk** or the Ponnani (Bharatapuzha) **river** — never the port town |
| Tanur | Absent from all 59 volumes |
| Kadalundi | Absent. Beypore appears once (1975–76) in a Palaeolithic survey; Chaliyam once (1965–66) for a 1355 AD mosque inscription |
| Koyilandy | Absent. "Quilandy" appears 3× — rock-cut caves with megalithic Black-and-red Ware (1962–63, 1964–65) and a 1747 AD epitaph |

The decisive entry is **1978–79, no. 40**: P. Narayana Babu and M. Raman
Namboodiri of the ASI Southern Circle explored Malappuram and Palghat districts
*"while investigating for the sites showing Roman contact in the Ponnani
valley."* They found megalithic topikal burials at Alancode, Ongallur,
Ponumundum, Tennala, Thannairkod and Thavanur, menhirs near Thirunavaya and
Thavanur, and rock-cut burials. **No Roman material.**

This matters more than simple absence. Somebody went looking for exactly the
thing the Tyndis identification predicts, in exactly the right place, and did
not find it. That is a negative result, not a gap.

One qualification worth keeping: **1970–71, no. 26** reports Russet-coated
(wavy line) Painted Ware in Taluk Ponnani, a ware that "overlaps with the
megalithic Black-and-red Ware in the early centuries of the Christian era."
The compiler calls it "of great significance." So the Ponnani valley was
occupied at the right date; what is missing is imported material.

### Nelkynda and Bakarē

Niranam, Neendakara, Purakkad, Pirakkad, Porakad, Thevalakara: **absent from all
59 volumes.** Kallada appears once (2006–07) for a neolithic axe from the river
basin. Kollam and Quilon appear only at district level, and the finds are
megalithic, mesolithic and neolithic.

Not one of the seven candidates for these two ports has ever been excavated.

### Muziris

**1969–70, no. 18.** The ASI Southern Circle with the Kerala Department of
Archaeology, under K.V. Soundara Rajan with K.V. Raman and N.G. Unnithan,
excavated in and around **Cranganore** — Karuppadana, Kilattali, Mathilakam,
Tirukkulasekharapuram and Tiruvanjikulam. Everything recovered was **ninth to
eleventh century**, Chera period. The following year (1970–71, no. 26) records
that the trial digs "had not yielded any tangible evidence."

This is the archaeological reason Cranganore lost its claim to Muziris. The
traditional identification was tested, and the ground returned material eight
centuries too late.

**2008–09, no. 29** is the official ASI record of Pattanam: 10° 09.434′ N,
76° 12.587′ E; 45-hectare core; Period II Early Historic, 2nd c. BCE – 4th c.
CE, with Roman amphora, terra sigillata, West Asian pottery, Turquoise Glazed
Pottery, Roman glass, cameo blanks and early Chera coins. Among the stated
objectives: *"locate Muziris the ancient trade emporium on the west coast."*

## What this settles, and what it does not

**Settles.** The earlier finding — that the material layer cannot discriminate
among the Kerala candidates — is confirmed and now rests on the complete
national record rather than on my library. For Nelkynda and Bakarē this is pure
absence of investigation. For Tyndis it is stronger: a targeted search for
Roman contact in the Ponnani valley that came back negative.

**Does not settle.** Absence in IAR is not absence in the ground. Ponnani,
Kodungallur and Kollam are living towns built over whatever lies beneath, and
Pattanam itself was found only because a modern brick-quarry exposed it. The
1969–70 Cranganore excavations reached 9th–11th century levels; whether anyone
went deeper is not stated.

The honest formulation for the thesis: **for Tyndis, Nelkynda and Bakarē the
material record is not silent because the evidence is ambiguous, but because
the excavation has not happened.** Any argument about their location has to be
carried by the textual distances and the coastal geomorphology, with the
material layer doing no more than confirming that the chosen candidate is not
contradicted.
