# What the sources actually say, port by port

Passages pulled from the library and the *Indian Archaeology: A Review* run,
filtered to those that make an identification, give a reason, report a find, or
flag a problem. 1,009 passages extracted; the ones below are the load-bearing
ones. Everything in quotation marks is verbatim.

---

## First, a finding that affects three ports at once

**The Periplus does not say the Malabar distances were sailed along the coast.
It says they were "by river and sea."**

> "Muziris, of the same Kingdom, abounds in ships sent there with cargoes from
> Arabia, and by the Greeks; it is located on a river, distant from Tyndis **by
> river and sea** five hundred stadia, and up the river from the shore twenty
> stadia." — Schoff 1912

> "Nelcynda is distant from Muziris **by river and sea** about five hundred
> stadia." — Schoff 1912

The Greek is *ὁμοίως διά τε ποταμοῦ καὶ διὰ θαλάσσης*, quoted by De Romanis.
Schoff draws the obvious inference:

> "The text tells us that Muziris was distant from Tyndis, 'by river and sea,
> 500 stadia,' and Nelcynda from Muziris, by river and sea, 500 stadia.' **This
> can hardly refer to anything but the Cochin backwaters.**"

This matters a great deal. Every distance measurement I have made for Tyndis,
Muziris and Nelkynda, and every one I have seen in the modern literature, uses
either a straight line or an open-sea coastal track. The text is describing a
route that runs partly through the Vembanad backwater system. The correct
measurement follows the channels, and the channels have moved.

It also explains an inconsistency I had treated as fatal. I previously found
that no Nelkynda and Bakarē pair satisfies both the 500-stadia and 120-stadia
notices. That conclusion assumed straight-line distance from Muziris. If the
500 stadia runs through the backwaters, the straight-line figure will
understate it, and candidates I had excluded as "too far south" may be at the
correct distance along the water.

**This is now the single most important thing to compute properly.**

## A second finding: Casson's own table is internally inconsistent

Casson prints a table of identifications with the distance in nautical miles
between each port and the next (Appendix 1, Table II). Two of those legs are
given in the Periplus as 500 stadia each:

| Leg, as Casson identifies it | Nautical miles | Stated stadia | Implied stadion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tyndis (Ponnani) to Muziris (Cranganore) | 39 | 500 | **144 m** |
| Muziris (Cranganore) to Nelkynda/Bakarē (Niranom–Pirakkad) | 55 | 500 | **204 m** |

The text gives the two legs as equal. Casson's identifications make the second
41 per cent longer than the first. One stadion value cannot satisfy both.

He is aware of the strain on the northern leg and says so:

> "Schoff puts Tyndis at Ponnani, which is **somewhat short of 500 stades**,
> while others put it at Kadalundi near Beypore (11°10'N), which is **somewhat
> over**; the odds are slightly in favor of Ponnani."

Three readings are possible, and they are not exclusive.

1. One or more of his identifications is wrong.
2. The two legs ran through different amounts of backwater, so the sailed
   distances differ even though the coastal distances do not.
3. **The author was not being precise.** This is the reading our own measurement
   supports: 82 per cent of the stadia figures in the Periplus are multiples of
   100, and every value at or above 200 is a round hundred without exception.
   "Five hundred stadia" looks like the author's round figure for a leg of
   roughly this length, applied to both without distinguishing a 41 per cent
   difference.

If the third reading is right, then the 500-stadia notices cannot bear the
weight the literature places on them, and the short unround figures, the 20
stadia at Muziris and the 120 at Nelkynda, are the more reliable numbers in the
passage. That inverts the usual order of argument.

## Tyndis

### What the text says
Five hundred stadia north of Muziris, by river and sea. Described as "a village
in plain sight on the shore."

### Who has proposed what

> "The exact location of the port is still unknown; however, scholars have
> tried to identify this place with either modern day **Kadalundi or Ponnani or
> Pantalayani Kollam**." — Dayalan 2018

> "It is described as a village in plain sight on the shore, and may be
> identified with the modern **Ponnani** (10° 48' N., 75° 56' E.)." — Schoff 1912

> "But the distance of 500 stadia between Tyndis and Muziris indicates
> **Ponnani**." — Schoff 1912

> "there is fairly good evidence for locating Muziris near Cranganore, **Tyndis
> near Ponnani or Beypore**, Bakare near Pirakkad with Nelkynda some twelve
> miles inland." — Casson 1989, Appendix 1

> "Less important was Tyndis (= Tamil **Toṇḍi** = Ponnāni?), located some 500
> stadioi north of Muziris, and Naoura (= Tamil Naravu), presumably further
> north." — De Romanis

### The argument from the Chera border

> "Ships sailing down the coast came first to the Chera kingdom, whose northern
> border was just above Tyndis and whose southern was somewhere between Muziris
> and Nelkynda, in modern terms, **north of Ponnani and south of Cranganore**."
> — Casson 1989

This is an independent constraint and it is usually overlooked. Tyndis sits at
or just inside a political boundary, not merely at a distance.

### What has been found
Nothing of Periplus date at any candidate. The one piece of positive
information is a negative result:

> IAR 1978–79, no. 40: the ASI Southern Circle explored Malappuram and Palghat
> "while investigating for the sites showing **Roman contact in the Ponnani
> valley**," and recorded megalithic topikal burials at Alancode, Ongallur,
> Ponumundum, Tennala, Thannairkod and Thavanur, and menhirs near Thirunavaya.

### Inconsistency to resolve
Casson allows Ponnani *or Beypore*, which are 40 km apart. Schoff commits to
Ponnani on the distance. De Romanis marks it with a question mark. Nobody
measures the distance through the backwaters, which is what the text specifies.

## Nelkynda and Bakarē

These must be treated as a pair. Nelkynda lies 500 stadia from Muziris by river
and sea; Bakarē is its harbour, 120 stadia downriver at the mouth.

> "the river Baris, must be considered together with its harbor, Bakare, which
> lay **120 stades downriver at the mouth**." — Casson 1989, Appendix 1

### The competing pairings

> "**Schoff identifies Nelkynda with Kottayam** (9°35'N, 76°31'E) on the
> Minachil River **and Bakare with Pirakkad** on the coast (9°21'N)."
> — Casson 1989, reporting Schoff

> "This place, distant 120 stadia from Nelcynda, at an inlet of the sea, can be
> no other than **Porakad** (9° 22' N., 76° 22' E.) for which it is a close
> transliteration; while **the distance from Kottayam is exactly in accord with
> the text**." — Schoff 1912

Schoff's claim of exact accord does not survive measurement. Kottayam to
Purakkad is 29.6 km straight line, against 18.6 km at a 155 m stadion or 22.2 km
at 185 m. Either Schoff measured loosely or he was using a much larger stadion.

> "The suggestion that Nelkynda was **Niranom (Neranom)**, which is about 500
> stades from Muziris and which is **on the Pambiyar twelve miles east of
> Pirakkad**, at least puts the port of trade and its harbor **on the same
> stream and the proper distance from each other**." — Casson 1989

This is Casson's own reasoning and it is the strongest version of the pair
argument. Twelve miles is 19.3 km, which is almost exactly 120 stadia at 155 m.
My own measurement of Niranam to Purakkad gives 21.2 km.

> "The consensus is that all are versions of **Kuttanadu** ... the area that
> includes the valley of the **Pambiyar**, noted for its fine pepper; **both
> Nelkynda and Bakare very likely stood on the Pambiyar**." — Casson 1989

> "Becare-Nelkynda, located on a river that flowed **less than 500 stadioi
> south of Muziris**, pointing to a place in **the southern part of Vembanad
> Lake**." — De Romanis

### Candidates I did not have

Dayalan lists identifications absent from our candidate table:

> "Other possible identifications of this place are with the modern **Kallada or
> Neendakara or Nirkunnam or Kannetri or Kollam**, all in south Kerala."

> "Bakare or Becare, the shipping port of Nelcynda at the mouth of the river, is
> identified by scholars either with modern **Markari or Varakkai** or it may be
> between **Kanetti and Kollam**."

> "**Nakkada** near Niranam in Pathanamthitta District, Kerala, is often
> identified with Nelcynda."

Nirkunnam, Kannetri, Markari, Varakkai and Nakkada need locating and adding.

### How confident is the field?

> "Five names — Semylla, **Muziris, Bakare**, Red Mountain, Komar — can be
> identified with more confidence than the others." — Casson 1989

Casson treats Bakarē as among the *securer* identifications, which is at odds
with how unsettled the modern discussion looks.

### The standing caveat
> "A further complication is that **extensive changes have taken place in the
> coastline**." — Casson 1989

## Muziris

### Why it is treated as secure

> "Muziris, which shared with Nelkynda the distinction of being one of the two
> key ports of the Malabar coast, **is securely located, thanks to mention in
> Tamil literature**: a poem ... talks of the city where the beautiful vessels,
> the masterpieces of the Yavanas, **stir white foam on the Periyar**, river of
> Kerala." — Casson 1989

Note what this secures and what it does not. The Tamil poem places Muziris on
the Periyar. It does not place it at Pattanam rather than Kodungallur, which are
9 km apart on the same delta.

### The older error, worth remembering
> "The earlier identification of Muziris and Nelcynda placed them at **Mangalore
> and Nileshwar**." — Schoff 1912

Those are 300 km north of the modern consensus. The identification has moved a
long way before.

### What has been found
Pattanam: 6,029 Roman amphora sherds, 122 terra sigillata, 3,098 torpedo jar,
8,534 Rouletted Ware, against 3,537,464 local sherds. Kodungallur: excavated at
five localities in 1969–70, everything 9th–11th century Chera.

## Barbarikon

### The field does not regard this as settled

> "Smith prudently observes that 'the **extensive changes which have occurred in
> the rivers of Sind** during the course of eighteen centuries **preclude the
> possibility of satisfactory identifications** of either of these towns
> [Barbarikon and Minnagar].'" — Casson 1989

> "Even though **there is no general agreement among scholars**, the location
> and the imposing structures of the fortified citadel of Banbhore on the Indus'
> delta led us at least to pose the question of its identification with
> Daybul/Debol." — Felici et al.

Note that Felici hedges even on *Daybul*, the Islamic identification, which is
far better attested than the Roman one.

> "Banbhore on the basis of its location and discovery of RPW from its earliest
> levels, **could be identified** with the famed port city of Barbarikon."
> — Mughal 2018

Mughal writes "could be." His editor's preface says the study "establishes and
identifies." The hedge belongs to the author.

### The excavation is incomplete for a physical reason
> "The layers below yielded a large collection of vessels (and some coins as
> well) attributed by Khan to the 'Scytho-Parthian' period. But **the virgin
> soil was not reached due to heavy water infiltration**, and information on the
> early stages of life at Banbhore remained incomplete." — Felici et al.

### Minnagar is a separate and unsolved problem
The Periplus ties Barbarikon to an inland capital, Minnagar, to which cargoes
were taken upriver. Cunningham put Minnagar near Tatta; Müller at Indore; Vincent
Smith at Madhyamika near Chitor; McCrindle and Fabricius in Kathiawar.
McCrindle cites three further identifications. These are hundreds of kilometres
apart. **Locating Barbarikon and locating Minnagar are the same problem, and the
second is in worse shape than the first.**

### The exact citation we still need
> Fazal Ahmad Khan, 'Excavations at Banbhore', *Pakistan Archaeology* 1 (1964),
> pp. 48–55.

*Pakistan Archaeology* is online at doam.gov.pk. Volume 1 should be obtainable.

## What to do next, in order

1. **Measure the Malabar distances through the backwaters, not across the sea.**
   The text specifies "by river and sea." This affects Tyndis, Muziris,
   Nelkynda and Bakarē simultaneously, and may dissolve the pairing
   inconsistency I reported earlier.
2. **Add the missing candidates**: Nakkada, Nirkunnam, Kannetri, Markari,
   Varakkai, and Beypore as a separate point from Kadalundi.
3. **Add the Chera border constraint** for Tyndis: north of Ponnani, south of
   Cranganore, per Casson.
4. **Treat Minnagar as a second locational problem** rather than an aside.
5. Read the Leukē Kōmē and Rhapta passage sets.
