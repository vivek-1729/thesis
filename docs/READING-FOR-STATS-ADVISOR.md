# Statistical methods, with published precedents in reconstructing the past

Each paper is a method the thesis needs, shown somewhere it has already been
applied to a question about the ancient world, so the reference carries both the
technique and the evidence that it transfers.

---

## Send these five

**1. Geostatistical inference under preferential sampling; Diggle, Menezes and Su (2010)**

How to do spatial inference when the places that were sampled were chosen because of what people expected to find there.

*JRSS Series C* 59, 191-232. doi:10.1111/j.1467-9876.2009.00701.x

**2. Modeling sea-level change using errors-in-variables integrated Gaussian processes; Cahill, Kemp, Horton and Parnell (2015)**

How to reconstruct a past physical surface from dated proxies while carrying the dating uncertainty all the way into the answer.

*Annals of Applied Statistics* 9, 547-571. doi:10.1214/15-AOAS824 · https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.6761

**3. Approximate Bayesian Computation of radiocarbon and paleoenvironmental record shows population resilience on Rapa Nui; DiNapoli, Crema, Lipo, Rieth and Hunt (2021)**

How to fit a simulation of the past to real archaeological data when the model has no tractable likelihood.

*Nature Communications* 12. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-24252-z

**4. A simple monotone process with application to radiocarbon-dated depth chronologies; Haslett and Parnell (2008)**

How to turn a sediment core into a dated sequence with honest error bars.

*JRSS Series C* 57, 399-418. doi:10.1111/j.1467-9876.2008.00623.x

**5. A probabilistic framework for assessing spatio-temporal point patterns in the archaeological record; Crema, Bevan and Lake (2010)**

How to analyse the distribution of finds when both where and when each one belongs are uncertain.

*Journal of Archaeological Science* 37. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0305440309004622

## The rest

**6. Large-scale, multi-temporal remote sensing of palaeo-river networks; Orengo and Petrie (2017)**

How to recover vanished river channels from satellite imagery and report them as probabilities rather than as lines on a map.

*Remote Sensing* 9. https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/9/7/735

**7. Model selection in historical research using approximate Bayesian computation; Rubio-Campillo (2016)**

How to choose between competing historical explanations rather than fitting only one.

*PLoS ONE* 11. https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0146491

**8. The frontier of simulation-based inference; Cranmer, Brehmer and Louppe (2020)**

The standard reference for inference when you can simulate from a model but cannot write down its likelihood.

*PNAS* 117. doi:10.1073/pnas.1912789117

**9. Points, patterns, and predictions in archaeological settlement data; Bilotti, Reindel, Isla and Mader (2025)**

How to model where sites are as a function of the landscape around them.

*Journal of Archaeological Science* 183. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0305440325002341

**10. Entropy-based methods to address sampling bias in archaeological predictive modeling; Çadırcı and Shahtahmassebi (2025)**

The archaeological statement of the sampling bias problem, and where that literature currently stands.

arXiv:2508.02272. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.02272

**11. End-to-end Bayesian analysis for summarizing sets of radiocarbon dates; Price, Capriles, Hoggarth, Bocinsky, Ebert and Jones (2021)**

How to combine many individually uncertain dates without creating patterns that are not there.

*Journal of Archaeological Science* 135, 105473. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0305440321001436

**12. Looking at the big picture: using spatial statistical analyses to study indigenous settlement patterns in the north-western Dominican Republic; Herrera Malatesta, Ulloa Hung and Hofman (2023)**

A clear worked example of fitting point process models to settlement data, freely available.

*Journal of Computer Applications in Archaeology* 6, 16-28. https://journal.caa-international.org/articles/10.5334/jcaa.83

**13. Was Thebes necessary? Contingency in spatial modelling; Evans and Rivers (2017)**

How much of a trade network's shape is forced by geography and how much of it is accident.

*Frontiers in Digital Humanities* 4:8. doi:10.3389/fdigh.2017.00008

## If only three

Diggle et al. 2010 for the identification problem, Cahill et al. 2015 for the
reconstruction machinery, and DiNapoli et al. 2021 for simulation-based
inference on archaeological data. Between them they cover the three statistical
problems the thesis actually has.
