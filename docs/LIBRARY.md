# Library

Everything held locally, and what to read to understand each port.

The library lives at `Thesis/library/`, outside the git repository because it is
about 620 MB of PDFs. Extracted text for every PDF is cached in
`library/text-cache/` with page markers, which is what the mining scripts read.

| Folder | Files | Size | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ancient-sources/` | 6 | 128 MB | The Periplus in Schoff and Casson, Ptolemy, Cosmas |
| `excavation-reports/` | 23 | 251 MB | Site reports for the excavated ports |
| `scholarship/` | 67 | 178 MB | Modern articles and monographs |
| `reference/` | 2 | 7 MB | Barrington Atlas directory, Ptolemy apparatus |
| `oa-harvest/` | 12 | 13 MB | Open-access harvest from the Crossref sweep |
| `iar-text/` | 78 | 29 MB | *Indian Archaeology: A Review*, 1953–54 to 2013–14 |
| `text-cache/` | 98 | 14 MB | Extracted text layers, page-marked |

Every PDF now carries a usable text layer. `McCrindle-1885-Ancient-India-as-
Described-by-Ptolemy.pdf`, 398 pages and the standard English commentary on
Ptolemy's India, was an image-only scan carrying 2,941 characters; it has been
OCR'd and now carries 587,000. The only remaining thin file is
`apa-IndOc-Gulf-IndianToponyms.pdf`, which is two pages.

## What to read, by port

Start with the two items under "read first" regardless of which port you are
working on. Everything else is per-port. Items marked **[missing]** are not held
and are listed again in the acquisitions section below.

### Read first, for any port

1. `ancient-sources/Casson-1989-Periplus-Maris-Erythraei-text-translation-commentary.pdf`
   — the standard edition. The commentary on the relevant chapter, plus
   Appendix 1 (harbours and ports) and Appendix 2 (distances), is where nearly
   every modern identification originates.
2. `scholarship/Tomber-2008-Indo-Roman-Trade-From-Pots-to-Pepper.pdf` — the ware
   typology. Without it, "Roman pottery at X" in the older Indian reports cannot
   be compared to anything.

`ancient-sources/Schoff-1912-Periplus-of-the-Erythraean-Sea.pdf` is the older
translation and is still worth reading for the Malabar ports, because several
identifications still in circulation are his and his reasoning is explicit.

### Leukē Kōmē

- `scholarship/Nappo-2010-JRA-23-On-the-Location-of-Leuke-Kome.pdf` — the
  standard modern argument, and the one we have found a measurement error in.
- `excavation-reports/Gawlikowski-Juchniewicz-al-Zahrani-2021-Aynuna-Nabataean-Port-Red-Sea.pdf`
  — the full Aynuna excavation, 450 pp. The coin catalogue is the decisive
  evidence.
- `excavation-reports/apa-RedSea-Aynuna-Juchniewicz2018.pdf` and
  `apa-RedSea-Aynuna-Gawlikowski2022.pdf` — shorter interim reports.
- `scholarship/apa-RedSea-LeukeKome-Fiema2020.pdf` — the al-Wajh side.
- `scholarship/apa-RedSea-Aynunah-Burton1878.pdf` — Burton's survey, the earliest
  description of the site.
- `scholarship/apa-RedSea-RedSea-Nappo2018.pdf` — the Red Sea trade context.
- **[missing]** ATLAL 29 (2020), Fiema et al., the al-Wajh–al-ʿUla survey. The
  only systematic survey covering the al-Wajh candidate.

### Barbarikon

- `excavation-reports/Felici-et-al-Banbhore-Pakistani-Italian-Excavations.pdf` —
  the current excavation, and the source of the water-table problem.
- `excavation-reports/` *Pakistan Archaeology* 33 (2018), Mughal on the
  Scytho-Parthian Red Polished Ware from the earliest levels.
- `scholarship/Ghosh-Barbarikon-in-the-Maritime-Trade-Network-of-Early-India.pdf`
- `scholarship/apa-IndOc-Gulf-Indus-Kervran1995.pdf` — the delta's changing
  channels, which is the real obstacle here.
- `scholarship/apa-IndOc-Gulf-Barygaza-Romanis2025.pdf` — the neighbouring port,
  and the northern anchor for any distance argument.
- **[missing]** F. A. Khan, *Banbhore: A Preliminary Report*, the original
  1958–66 report describing the Scytho-Parthian coins.

### Tyndis

- `scholarship/Dayalan-2018-Ancient-Seaports-Western-Coast-India.pdf` — the
  fullest recent list of proposed identifications for all the Malabar ports.
- `scholarship/Historical-Archaeology-Iron-Age-Early-Historic-Kerala.pdf` — the
  regional archaeological background.
- `scholarship/Medieval-Ports-Maritime-Activities-North-Malabar.pdf` and
  `Re-Inventing-Panthalayani-Kollam-heritage.pdf` — the later-harbour evidence
  that put Koyilandy and its neighbours on the candidate list in the first place.
- `iar-text/` 1970–71 and 1978–79 — the only fieldwork in this area that bears on
  the question, positive and negative respectively.

### Nelkynda and Bakarē

- Casson Appendix 1 on the pairing, and Schoff on Kottayam and Purakkad. These
  are the two competing reconstructions and both are in the ancient-sources PDFs.
- `scholarship/apa-IndOc-Gulf-MuzirisPapyrus-Romanis2015.pdf` and
  `apa-IndOc-Gulf-IndiaTrade-Romanis2015.pdf` — De Romanis on the southern
  Vembanad reading.
- `scholarship/Cobb-2018-Rome-and-the-Indian-Ocean-Trade-introduction.pdf`
- `scholarship/Dayalan-2018` again, for the five candidates we do not yet have
  located: Nakkada, Nirkunnam, Kannetri, Markari, Varakkai.

### Muziris

- `excavation-reports/Cherian-2011-Pattanam-5th-Season-Field-Report.pdf` — the
  quantified finds tables. This is the only candidate anywhere with
  season-by-season published counts.
- `excavation-reports/PAMA-Unearthing-Pattanam-Excavation-Catalogue.pdf`
- `scholarship/Shajan-Tomber-Selvakumar-Cherian-2004-JRA-Locating-the-Ancient-Port-of-Muziris.pdf`
- `scholarship/apa-IndOc-Gulf-MuzirisPapyrus-Romanis2012.pdf` and `2014` — the
  Vienna papyrus, the best documentary evidence for the trade itself.
- `ancient-sources/Casson-1990-ZPE-New-Light-on-Maritime-Loans-Muziris-Papyrus.pdf`
- `scholarship/Gurukkal-2001-JRA-In-Search-of-Muziris.pdf` — Gurukkal and
  Whittaker's field reconnaissance, which confirms Kodungallur and narrows it to
  the Periyar-Pullut confluence. OCR'd from an image-only scan.

### Rhapta

- `scholarship/apa-IndOc-Gulf-Rhapta-Chami1998.pdf`, `Chami1999.pdf`,
  `Chami2002.pdf`, and
  `Chami-1999-Graeco-Roman-link-Rufiji-CurrentAnthropology-40-2.pdf` — the Rufiji
  case. The last is the load-bearing one and the most cited archaeological claim
  about Rhapta.
- `scholarship/Hughes-2016-GIS-Approach-Metropolis-of-Rhapta.pdf` — the only
  prior quantitative attempt at this problem, on any port.
- `scholarship/apa-IndOc-Gulf-Zanzibar-Juma2004.pdf` — 200 pp on Unguja Ukuu, and
  the source of the unreferenced claim about early Roman coins north of the
  Rufiji.
- `scholarship/apa-IndOc-Gulf-Mafia-Crowther2014.pdf`, `Christie2017.pdf`,
  `Ryano2021.pdf` — the Mafia candidate.
- `scholarship/Chami-2021-Geographical-Extent-of-Azania.pdf` and
  `apa-RedSea-PeriplusMarisErythraei-Robin1991.pdf` — the Azania question.
- `excavation-reports/apa-IndOc-Gulf-Somalia-Chittick1976.pdf` and `1979.pdf`
- **[missing]** Datoo 1970, *Azania* 5, 65–75. The origin of the Pangani–Rufiji
  bracket that every later argument inherits. Still being cited at second hand.

### Comparative anchors: the securely located ports

These matter because they are the calibration set. What a confirmed Periplus port
looks like materially is defined by these, not by the disputed ones.

- `excavation-reports/apa-RedSea-MyosHormos-PeacockandBlue2011.pdf` (387 pp)
- `excavation-reports/Sidebotham-2011-Berenike-and-the-Ancient-Maritime-Spice-Route.pdf`
  and `apa-RedSea-Berenike-Sidebotham2020.pdf`
- `excavation-reports/apa-IndOc-Gulf-Sumhuram-Avanzini2011.pdf` and `2014.pdf`
- `excavation-reports/apa-IndOc-Gulf-Qana-Sedov1997.pdf`
- `excavation-reports/apa-IndOc-Gulf-Arikamedu-Pillai2010.pdf`
- `excavation-reports/apa-IndOc-Gulf-RasHafun-Smith1988.pdf`
- `excavation-reports/apa-IndOc-Gulf-Socotra-Jansen2018.pdf`
- `excavation-reports/apa-RedSea-Adulis-Zazzaro2021.pdf`

### Method and context, not port-specific

- `scholarship/apa-RedSea-PeriplusMarisErythraei-Arnaud2012.pdf` — on how the
  Periplus measures distance, which bears directly on the stadion work.
- `scholarship/apa-RedSea-RedSeaWinds-Whitewright2007.pdf` — rigging and sailing
  performance, the input to any voyage simulation.
- `scholarship/ORBIS-Scheidel-Meeks-geospatial-network-model.pdf` — the nearest
  existing model of ancient travel as a network.
- `scholarship/apa-IndOc-Gulf-IndianOcean-Seland2014.pdf` — the standard survey.
- `scholarship/apa-IndOc-Gulf-India-SLR-Nigam2024.pdf` — sea-level change on the
  Indian coast, which is the geomorphology layer.

## Still to acquire

In priority order. The first three are arguments currently being taken on trust.

1. **Datoo 1970**, "Rhapta: the Location and Importance of East Africa's First
   Port", *Azania* 5, 65–75. doi:10.1080/00672707009511528. Not open access;
   Harvard has Taylor & Francis.
2. **Turner 1989**, *Roman Coins from India*. Appendix 1 catalogues every Roman
   coin find in India **including single and stray finds**, which is the category
   CHRE omits and the one that actually bears on harbour location. Routledge
   reissue 9780367605827.
4. **Salles & Sedov (eds)**, *Qāni'. Le port antique du Hadramawt*, Brepols 2010,
   553 pp. Several hundred bronze coins; would give a second quantified anchor
   assemblage beside Pattanam and a second bronze site-find assemblage beside
   Aynuna's 25. Print only.
5. **Suresh 2004**, *Symbols of Trade*, Manohar, ISBN 9788173045523. The
   systematic collation of Roman objects in India.
6. **F. A. Khan**, *Banbhore: A Preliminary Report*, Department of Archaeology and
   Museums, Pakistan. Other volumes of *Pakistan Archaeology* should be swept
   from doam.gov.pk at the same time.
7. **ATLAL** back volumes 1–26, for the north-west Arabian coast before the
   Saudi–Polish mission. No complete open archive found.

Two categories appear not to exist in accessible form at all. Kerala State
Department of Archaeology excavation reports as a series: only Pattanam is
published accessibly, and if reports exist for Kollam, Niranam or Purakkad they
are not on the open web. And a complete open ATLAL archive.
