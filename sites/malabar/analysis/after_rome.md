# After Rome: Pattanam and the Periyar mouth, 2nd to 15th century

*Research notes for reworking Part 4, argument 3, of `ceramics_and_ports.md`. Prepared 10 November 2026. This is a collection of evidence, not the rewrite of the argument. It was assembled by a research agent: **[Read]** here means the agent read the source, not that it has been re-checked by hand. Citations to the local readings were spot-checked (Cherian 2012: 27, 28).*

---

## The question, and why it matters for the thesis

Argument 3 currently says that Pattanam's largest Gulf assemblage, the torpedo jars and the turquoise glazed ware, almost certainly postdates the Periplus, and that the site's "Early Historic" period (300 BC to AD 500) is too long to show otherwise. The proposal is to turn the argument round and make it about the period after the Roman peak. The questions are these:

1. What happened at Pattanam after the first century AD? When does its Early Historic phase end, is there a gap, and what does the medieval phase contain?
2. What happened in the surrounding region, meaning the Periyar mouth, Kodungallur (Cranganore) and the central Kerala coast?
3. Do the texts and the archaeology show a decline after Rome, or a port zone that carried on through Gulf, Sasanian, early Islamic and Asian networks?

This bears on the locating problem in two ways. First, if the Gulf material at Pattanam belongs to the 3rd to 8th centuries, it shows that the site remained a port while the texts stopped naming Muziris. A long-lived port is evidence of a good harbour, but not of a harbour *in AD 50*. Second, the later history explains why Kodungallur was the traditional candidate. It was a real port and royal centre in the 9th to 15th centuries, and the older scholarship read that later prominence back onto Muziris.

The identification of Pattanam with Muziris is disputed, and nothing here assumes it. Where a source makes the equation, the note says so.

## The answer in brief

1. **Pattanam's own sequence is thin after about AD 100, and there is no dating evidence for it at all.** Every published radiocarbon date from the Early Historic layers falls between about 90 BC and AD 125. The "Medieval" period (AD 500 to 1500) is a thousand-year unit that the excavators themselves say they cannot subdivide. They describe it with "a few medieval roof tiles", "reuse of bricks", and "very little evidence for trade activity between 1000 CE and 1500 CE". The catalogue goes further than the reports: "even nil for five centuries after 1000 CE".
2. **Gulf contact plausibly continued at Pattanam into the 8th century or later, but this rests on the date ranges of whole classes, not on any dated deposit.** Kennet's reading of the 2007 turquoise glazed ware is "between the 3rd/2nd century BCE and the 7th/8th century CE". Tomber dated one glazed sherd to the 8th to 10th centuries. Antiquity 2008 refers to "Early Medieval (AD 500–1000)" contexts with possible Gulf fabrics. Seland reports late Roman Aqaba (Aila) amphorae of the 5th to 7th centuries, a claim I could not trace to its source.
3. **Muziris stops being named by the 6th century at the latest.** Ptolemy (2nd century) lists Mouziris as an *emporion*. The Peutinger Table shows it with a temple of Augustus, but cannot be dated. Cosmas (6th century) names the pepper country Male and its five marts, Parti, Mangarouth, Salopatana, Nalopatana and Poudopatana, and Muziris is not among them. In the 9th century the Arab route runs through Kulam (Kollam), not the Periyar mouth.
4. **In the 9th to 12th centuries the regional centre is Kodungallur (Mahodayapuram), not Pattanam.** The ASI's 1969–70 trial excavations around Cranganore reported deposits of the 9th to 11th centuries, with Chinese celadon and Chola coins at Mathilakam (Madilakam). The next year's IAR said those digs "had not yielded any tangible evidence relating to the second Chera empire". The Jewish copper plates (c. AD 1000) were issued while the king resided at Muyirikkodu.
5. **For the 13th to 15th centuries the evidence is textual and late.** It consists of Shingly in the Genizah and the Arab geographers, Odoric's Cyngilin, and Barbosa's Cranganor, a mixed Christian, Muslim and Hindu town where the king of Cochin "holds certain dues". The 1341 flood has no contemporary record. The rise of Kochi is well attested in 15th-century Chinese sources, but its cause is not.

## The landscape after Rome

**The 1341 flood.**

- The flood "opened up the present harbour of Kochi and the Vembanad backwater lake system to the sea", forming the Puthuvype, "the new deposit of land called Wypen (Vipen) island". Gurukkal and Whittaker add that "every book says" this, and that "geologically it was only the most spectacular of the physical changes" (Gurukkal and Whittaker 2001: 341). **[Supplementary]**
- No contemporary account of the flood survives. Its date rests on the Puthuvype era, which is reckoned from 1341 (Wikipedia, *1341 Kerala floods*). **[Secondhand]**
- Dames places the drying of the Cranganor channel "from the beginning of the fourteenth century" (Dames 1921, vol. 2: 89 n.). **[Read]**
- Against this, Vaz Dorado and Baldaeus show a working port at Kodungallur in the 16th and 17th centuries (above).

The event is best treated as a tradition about a gradual shift, which the 15th-century Chinese evidence for Kochi confirms in outcome but not in date or cause.

**What the sediments at Pattanam say.**

- The site stands on a palaeobeach ridge at 3.32 m above sea level. The Iron Age sand formed "by aeolian reworking of palaeo-beach sand subsequent to the late mid-Holocene regression" (Cherian et al. 2009: 237). **[Read]**
- The geoarchaeology abstract in the 2011 report: "The sediment column does not posses evidence of any natural catastrophic events and it is postulated that Pattanam was abandoned in Early Medieval period due to unknown reasons" (Cherian 2011: 31). **[Supplementary]** This is the excavators' geologist ruling out a flood horizon at the site, and so it cuts against the lead paper's link to 1341.
- In 2015 the groundwater stood at 266 cm and hampered excavation (Cherian 2015: 746). In 2020 a trench at Mathilakam was abandoned for the same reason (PAMA 2020: 2). **[Supplementary]** The water table is a constraint on dating the lower layers, not the upper ones.

**The channels and the coast.**

- Narayana, Priju and Chakrabarti 2001 identify a palaeodelta of about 50 km² near Munambam. They attribute the westward shift of the Periyar's channel to "neotectonism" and give no dates. **[Read]**
- Gurukkal and Whittaker report that the shore lay along the Tottapalli river "some 200–300 years ago" and along the Pullut river, about 6.5 km inland, "some 2000 years ago" (2001: 341). **[Supplementary]** The source for this is their note 28, which I have not followed up.
- Das et al. 2024 have cores at the Periyar mouth with a youngest age of about 1.1 cal kyr BP, around AD 850. The paper proposes a "three-stage evolutionary model" placing Muziris on the Periyar near Kodungallur (DOAJ abstract, and the project's `FINDINGS.md` §13). **[Secondhand]** Elsevier blocked the download. This is the only dated sediment from the Periyar mouth that falls in the post-Roman period.
- Padmalal et al. 2014 report, near Pattanam, "multiple lines of evidence of human habitation which were subsequently buried under fluvio-marine sediments in the Late Holocene" (search snippet). **[Secondhand]** If this holds, part of the later sequence could be buried off the mound rather than missing.

**Link to the quantitative strand.**

- Pattanam lies 0.22 km from the undated ancient shoreline in Narayana 2001 (`archive/docs/new_findings_v2.md` §6a).
- The 1857 Admiralty sheets record the Kodungallur mouth after Kochi's rise but before the 20th-century reclamation. If the Cranganore channel was still navigable in 1671 (Baldaeus), the 1857 soundings are closer to the late medieval channel than the modern water network is. That is a reason to transcribe the Kodungallur soundings first.
- None of the dated landscape evidence reaches the 1st century. Das et al.'s youngest age is about eight centuries too late.

## The debates

**1. Decline after Rome, against continuity.**

- **For decline at Pattanam.**
  - The radiocarbon dates stop around AD 125.
  - Mediterranean imports stop in the 2nd century (Seland 2014: 373). The Aqaba amphora claim is unconfirmed.
  - Cosmas names five Malabar marts and Muziris is not one of them (Tomber 2008: 165).
  - Late Roman coins are almost absent from Kerala (Darley 2014: 234).
  - The 9th-century Gulf route runs to Kollam.
  - The excavators' own phrase is "gradually declined after the 'glitter and glory' of the Muziris phase" (Cherian 2011: 4).
- **For continuity in the region.**
  - Gulf wares at Pattanam span the 3rd to 8th centuries, on Kennet's and Tomber's ranges.
  - Cosmas has a church in Male with a Persian connection.
  - Christian, Jewish and Muslim merchants were active under the Cheras from 849.
  - Kodungallur was a royal capital from the 9th century, with celadon and Chola coins reported near it.
  - Shingly was a Jewish town in the 12th to 14th centuries, and Cranganore was still a pepper entrepôt in 1516.

These are compatible. The evidence supports a regional port system that continued while its centre moved, and a Pattanam whose own role shrank after the 2nd or 3rd century. It does not support a single moment of collapse.

**2. Is the Pattanam gap real or an artefact?**

Several features of how the site was dug and published would produce a gap whether or not there was one:

- The excavators say the medieval layers cannot be differentiated stratigraphically "primarily due to lack of artifactual evidence" (Cherian 2011: 4). In the 2015 trench the modern and medieval layers together are only the top 50–60 cm (Cherian 2015: 745).
- Reports describe heavy disturbance. In PT10 XIX "pits could be observed from modern layers to medieval layers, sometimes cutting into early historic layers", and antiquities "of various periods are often found mixed up" (Cherian 2011: 24). The same report speaks of "continuous occupation of the site from Iron Age to the Modern times with yet to be confirmed gaps or desertion" (Cherian 2011: 24–25). **[Supplementary]**
- The phase boundaries are set by assumption: Early Historic to AD 500, Medieval from AD 500. No date anchors either boundary, and Gulf wares with ranges to the 8th century are counted as Early Historic.
- Less than 1 per cent of the mound has been dug, in 60 to 66 trenches (PAMA: 3; PAMA 2020: 1). **[Supplementary]**
- Local medieval pottery is not characterised in anything I have seen, so a late first-millennium layer could only be recognised by imports.

Against the gap being purely an artefact, the evidence for 1000–1500 is genuinely weak. The excavators say so in the catalogue, and the published Chinese ceramics are 16th century. The fairest reading is that **a 3rd-to-10th-century occupation is probably present but undated and lumped into "Early Historic", and that the 11th-to-15th-century gap may be real**. The second half of that sentence is the more secure.

## What this means for argument 3

**What the evidence supports.**

1. **The Gulf material at Pattanam probably belongs mostly to the period after the Periplus, and that period is when the texts stop naming Muziris.**
   - On the class ranges, the torpedo jars are 3rd century or later.
   - On Kennet's reading, the turquoise glazed ware may run to the 7th or 8th century.
   - Cosmas, the Akhbār and the Arab geographers do not name a port at the Periyar mouth.
   - So Pattanam's largest non-Mediterranean assemblage would come from a time when the site was textually invisible. This reframes the existing argument without changing its basis.
2. **The area remained a port zone, and its centre moved north.**
   - The 9th-to-11th-century evidence is at Kodungallur, Mathilakam and Tiruvanchikulam, not at Pattanam.
   - The 12th-to-14th-century Jewish town of Shingly was inland on the Periyar system, wherever exactly it was.
   - Cranganore was still a port in 1516 and 1671.
3. **The traditional identification of Muziris with Kodungallur rests largely on this later history.** Dames's "undoubtedly the Muziris of Pliny" (1921: 88 n.) reads the medieval Muyirikkodu back onto the 1st century.

**What the evidence does not support.**

- **Any date for the end of Pattanam's port activity.** There are no radiocarbon dates after AD 125, and no stratified sequence for the Gulf wares.
- **Any claim that Pattanam is or is not Muziris.** The absence of Muziris from Cosmas is an argument from silence, and Muyirikkodu is a name, not a location.
- **The 1341 flood as the cause of Pattanam's decline.** On every reading Pattanam's decline came centuries earlier, and the site's own sediments show no catastrophic event. The flood belongs to the Kodungallur–Kochi story, and even there it is a tradition.
- **"Continuity" in a strong sense at the site.** Continuity is regional, not demonstrated on the mound.

**A possible restatement of argument 3, for discussion:** Pattanam's Roman horizon is dated by radiocarbon and amphora types to the 1st century BC and the 1st century AD. Its Gulf horizon is dated only by class ranges that run into the 8th century. After that, the port zone of the Periyar mouth is visible at Kodungallur and in the texts, not at Pattanam. The "Early Historic" label hides the second of these horizons, and the first is the only one specific to the Periplus.

## Open leads and what to check

1. **The Aqaba amphorae at Pattanam.** Trace Seland's claim to its source (Tomber 2009? Selvakumar et al. 2009 in JIOA?). If present and counted, they are the only firmly post-AD 300 Red Sea import.
2. **Kennet's specialist report** on the Pattanam turquoise glazed ware and torpedo jars. This is the same lead as in argument 3. Cherian 2012: 27 cites Kennet's dating; find the report itself.
3. **Tomber 2015**, "The Roman pottery from Pattanam", in K. S. Mathew (ed.), *Imperial Rome, Indian Ocean Regions and Muziris* (New Delhi 2015): 381–394 **[Secondhand]**. This may say how late the Mediterranean material runs.
4. **Selvakumar, Gopi and Shajan 2005** (JIOA 2), the 2004 trial trench report with Period III, and Selvakumar, Shajan and Mahadevan 2006 (IJDL). The rmrl.in link is dead; try Academia or the IJDL.
5. **Sort the 480 Chinese sherds** by ware and date. Is any of it Song or Yuan celadon, as at Mathilakam in 1969–70?
6. **The ASI 1969–70 Cranganore material**, and why the 1970–71 IAR withdrew the claim. Also K. V. Raman's later publication of the Kodungallur excavations.
7. **Das et al. 2024** (*Quaternary Science Advances* 14: 100197). Get the PDF by another route. It holds the only post-Roman sediment dates at the Periyar mouth.
8. **Padmalal et al. 2014** (*Quaternary International*, 10.1016/j.quaint.2013.12.032), on the habitation buried under fluvio-marine sediment near Pattanam.
9. **Gurukkal and Whittaker 2001 n. 28**, the source of the Pullut and Tottapalli shorelines.
10. **The *Malabar Gazetteer*: 402–403**, which is the source of the 14th-century drying of the Cranganore channel in Dames.
11. **The Ravenna Cosmography** (c. AD 700). Potts 2017 (*Studia Iranica* 46) examines its Indian names. Does Muziris appear? An old note gives "Niloinna" for Nelkynda **[Secondhand]**.
12. **Mackintosh-Smith 2014**, *Accounts of China and India* (NYU), for an English check on the Ferrand passage on Kūlam Malay.
13. **Narayanan 2013**, *Perumāḷs of Kerala*, and Veluthat on Mahodayapuram, for the primary readings of the Kodungallur inscriptions.
