# Pattanam excavation reports: object-level notes

Sources read: the five supplementary PDFs in `sites/malabar/readings/supplementary/`, using the page-marked text in `analysis/text/supp_*.txt` and rendered pages in `analysis/renders/pattanam/` (150 dpi; every page carrying pottery images was viewed). Quotations are verbatim from the text layer, checked against the rendered page. Where I read text off an image that the text layer does not carry, I say so.

**The headline finding.** No illustrated pottery object in any of these five sources is given a trench, locus, layer, depth or accession number in its caption. The PAMA catalogue, the only source with many object photographs, is an exhibition catalogue. Its captions describe the ware class, not the individual sherd. Several sherds carry handwritten ink registration numbers, but the embedded photographs are about 72 dpi (for example, the image on PDF p. 29 is 357 x 509 px across 357 x 509 pt), and the numbers cannot be read even at 400 dpi re-rendering. Object-level provenance therefore has to come from the KCHR interim reports or the Pattanam database, which are not in this folder.

## 2. Site-level facts (verbatim, with pages)

### 2.1 Location and size

- **Coordinates and setting.** PAMA, PDF 12 (folio 12): "The Pattanam archaeological site (N. Lat. 10°09.434'; E. Long. 76°12.587') is located in Vadakkekara village of Paravur Taluk, about 25 km north of Kochi ... This is a coastal site located in the delta of the Periyar River and is now about 4 km from the Arabian Sea coast. The Paravur Todu, presently a distributory of the Periyar, flows about 1 km south of the site. The river Periyar flows 5 km north of Pattanam."
- **Height and area.** PAMA, PDF 12: "The Pattanam mound is 3.32 m high above Mean Sea Level (MSL) and is spread out over about 70 hectares."
- **Location in the 2004 article.** Shajan et al. 2004, p. 313: "Pattanam (76°10' E, 10°10' N) lies 1.5 km northwest of Paravur ... The site is located c.3.5 km east of the modern coast and c.4.5 km southeast of the mouth of the Periyar."
- **Extent in the 2004 article.** Shajan et al. 2004, p. 317: "a large concentration of surface finds ... spanning an area up to 1 square km." On p. 318 they add: "its size, up to 1 sq. km, would be sufficient to mark a major port."
- **Distances in 2012.** Cherian 2012, p. 23: "About one km south of the site flows the Paravur Todu, a tributary of the river Periyar which is about six km to the north of Pattanam. The Arabian Sea is about 4 km west of the site. About one km from the western boundary of the site is the Tattapally River or Munambam kayal".
- **Coordinates in the 2011 abstracts.**
  - Cherian 2011 (Rajesh and Cherian abstract, PDF 30): "Pattanam (N 10 09' 24.1", E 076 12' 32.9")".
  - Cherian 2011 (Dineesh Krishnan abstract, PDF 22), trench XVI: "(N 100 09' 24.4" E 0760 12' 32.7")". The text layer drops the degree sign.

### 2.2 Excavation extent and trenches

- **Coverage to 2014.** PAMA, PDF 7 (folio 3): "The KCHR has so far excavated 60 trenches at the Pattanam mound, covering less than 1% of the 70 hectares of the mound."
- **Trench PT 14 LI.** PAMA, PDF 16: "The trench size was 5 x 4 meters, and it took two months to reach the natural soil at a depth of 317 cm." The same page says the trench "had five cultural layers, representing the Modern, Medieval, Early Historic, Iron Age–Early Historic transition, and the Iron Age periods."
- **Section of Trench LI.** PAMA, PDF 18 (folio 24–25) has a section drawing of the eastern face of Trench LI. The datum is 0–375 cm and "Unexcavated" lies below about 3 m. The loci shown are 002, 005, 006, 011, 012, 013, 014, 031, 032, 036, 046, 048 and 053. The text reads: "in the sandy layer at 3 m are loci 048 and 053. The sand mixed with clay layer over this has locus 046 in it."
- **The 2011 season.** Cherian 2011, p. 1: "The excavation lasted four months (Feb to May 2011) and ten trenches, including open trenches covering a total area of ca. 250 sq m were dug in three phases. (Phase I: Trenches 28, 29, 30, 31; Phase II: Trenches 32, 33 and Phase III: Trenches 34, 35, 36, 37). The open- trenches were of 10 x 10 m and 9 x 4.5 m dimensions." The same page places the 2011 trenches "in the northwestern sector 500 m away from the area where a wharf structure and a ca. 2000-year-old canoe were excavated earlier."
- **Area to 2010.** Cherian 2011 (Sarathchandrababu et al. abstract, PDF 32): "The 650 square meter area excavated in the last four seasons (from 2007 to 2010) has produced three million body sherds and around two hundred thousand diagnostic sherds."
- **Trenches named in the 2011 abstracts:** PT XVI (2010, kiln or furnace feature, PDF 22); PT10 XIX (7 m NS x 4 m WE, heavily pitted, PDF 24); PT10 XVII (pits in all layers, PDF 30); PT XVIII (2010, 18 wooden pegs "at a depth of 4 meter", peat below the Iron Age layers, PDF 35).
- **Trench PT07 II.** Cherian 2012, Fig. 1 (p. 27), labelled "PT07 II", shows the sequence labelled from top to bottom: "Early Medieval ?", "Early Historic Layer", "Transition Layer", "Iron Age Layer", "Natural Sand Layer". The depth scale on the drawing is too small to read reliably, and I do not report depths from it.

### 2.3 Deposit depth and cultural sequence

- **PAMA's five periods.** PAMA, PDF 14 (folio 16): "The roughly 4 m of occupation deposit and layers have been divided into five cultural periods, based on the archaeological material within the strata as well as dates acquired through scientific techniques. The five periods are: Iron Age (c. 1000 BCE – 500 BCE) / Iron Age-Early Historic transition (c. 500 BCE – 300 BCE) / Early Historic (c. 300 BCE – 500 CE) / Medieval (c. 500 CE – 1500 CE) and / Modern (c. 1500 CE onwards)."
  - The roundel graphic on the same spread misprints the medieval range as "c. 500 CE – 1500 BCE" (read from the image).
- **What marks the transition period.** PAMA, PDF 14: "The second period represents the end of the Iron Age and the beginning of the Early Historic, and was prior to the trade across the Arabian Sea. Materials such as Iron Age ceramics and iron objects, as well as a piece of Russet Coated Pottery and fragments of Turquoise Glazed Pottery, suggest the transitional nature of this period."
- **The 2011 sequence.** Cherian 2011, p. 2: "The stratification of the 4m thick cultural deposit is explained below ... The upper stratum of about 1m.thickness, with a humus crest, represents the modern period (21st to 17th c CE). The next 2 to 2.5m. is the core stratum of Pattanam, representing the Post (?), Mid (?) and Early-Medieval (?) periods and the Early Historic period (16th c CE to 1st c BCE). Underneath the relatively undisturbed lower levels of Early Historic stratum is the 30 to 50 cm thick sandy layers representing the Early Historic - Iron Age transition (2nd c BCE to 4th c BCE) and Iron Age period (5th c BCE to 10th c BCE)."
- **Basis for the sequence.**
  - Cherian 2011, p. 2: "The stratigraphic layers and artefactual assemblages broadly validate with some question marks, the cultural sequence suggested in the previous seasons."
  - Cherian 2011, p. 2, on the medieval layers: "Stratigraphic differentiation of the medieval layers (16th c CE to 5th c CE) is difficult, primarily due to lack of artifactual evidence."
- **Radiocarbon for the Iron Age start.** Cherian 2012, p. 24: "High precision Accelerator Mass Spectrometry (AMS) Carbon14 (C14) dating on the charcoal samples from the aeolian sandy layers at depths varying from 340 cm to 370 cm confirm that native settlement had begun at Pattanam in the Iron Age phase (around 1000 BCE)." This is cited (n. 12) to the Interim Report 2009 and to Cherian et al. 2009, *Current Science* 97(2): 236–240.
- **Early Historic range in 2012.** Cherian 2012, p. [22]: "an early historic (1st c BCE – 5th c CE), multi-cultural port site".
- **Early Historic range in 2004.** Shajan et al. 2004, p. 313: "an Early Historic (c.300 B.C.-A.D. 300) settlement at Pattanam".
- **Disturbance.**
  - Cherian 2011, p. 2: "The intense disturbance of the site partially explains the highly fragmented artifacts".
  - Cherian 2011 (Jaseera Majeed et al. abstract, PDF 23): "Very few artifacts are recovered from the primary context, the possible exceptions being the architectural remains."
  - Cherian 2011 (Madhavi Kunneriath abstract, PDF 24): "Antiquities, ceramics and architectural debris of various periods are often found mixed up which underline the severity of disturbance."
  - This bears directly on how far any single sherd can be dated from its layer.
- **Depth used to date decorated pottery.** Cherian 2011 (Vasudevan et al. abstract, PDF 34): "The chronology of decorated pottery may be assumed as Early Historic period, since the majority of decorated sherds were from the depth ranging between 200-300 cms." This is a depth-based period assignment, not a measured date.

### 2.4 The wharf and canoe

- **PAMA's dating of the canoe.** PAMA, PDF 67 (folio 123): "In the 2007 Pattanam excavation, a highly decayed 6 m-long wooden dug-out canoe was found adjacent and parallel to the wharf like structure. The wood was identified to be Anjili (Artocarpushirsutus Lamk) ... The canoe and bollards, from the same context as the wharf, have been radiocarbon dated by leading laboratories in the USA and India: the University of Georgia and the Institute of Physics, Bhubaneswar. Recovered dates fell within the 1st century BCE – 1st century CE period. Hence, it may be assumed that the wharf could also belong to roughly the same period."
  - The caption on PDF 2 repeats this: "A view of the excavated wharf, with a wooden canoe and bollards radiocarbon dated to the period between the 1st century BCE – 1st century CE."
- **The wharf, from the 2012 abstract.** Cherian 2012, p. [22]: "The excavations in the northeastern part of the site revealed a wharf and a warehouse structure, a wooden canoe made of anjili in a waterlogged context, along with nine bollards of teak. The wharf was a platform made of a mixture of laterite, clay and lime, with an elaborate brick-lining where the reclining platform touches the water."
- **Dimensions and the calibrated dates.** Cherian 2012, p. 28: "The part of the wharf that could be excavated was a huge structure (+6m in length and 7.3m in width), made of a mixture of laterite granules and had a nineteen course outer brick-lining ... The intact portion of the highly decayed canoe measures ca 6 m + in length and 30 cm in breadth and the AMS dates put the C14 calibration age range from 1300 to 100 BCE (i.e.700± 600 cal. BCE with 95.4% probability). The AMS dates for the teak bollard sample puts the C14 calibrated age range from 1100 BCE to 1300 CE (i.e 100± 1200 cal. CE with 95.4% probability). The layer below the canoe had amphorae, rouletted ware, TGP and other ceramics, as a floor for the water-logged area of the wharf."
- **Botanical layer.** Cherian 2012, p. 28: "Beneath the ceramic layer of the water-logged area of the wharf, at a depth of 3m, was a rich collection of botanical remains with a layer of clay over them."

## 5. Interpretive claims by the authors

These are kept separate from the documented facts above.

### 5.1 Muziris identification (hedging varies)

- **Shajan et al. 2004:**
  - p. 318: "the arguments for equating Pattanam with ancient Muziris, or at least as part of the Muziris network, are strong."
  - p. 319: "It is here proposed that Pattanam was part of the legendary Muziris, although this needs to be tested by open-area excavations".
- **Cherian 2011:**
  - p. 1 states it outright: "this ancient port town (Muziris)".
  - The Nambirajan and Cherian abstract (PDF 26) is more cautious: "More research and evidences are necessary ... before anything could be said of this site being Muziris."
- **Cherian 2012, p. [22]:** "is now widely considered as an integral part of the ancient port of Muziris".
- **PAMA:**
  - PDF 6: "probably the ancient port of Muziris".
  - PDF 13: "could have been an integral part of the long lost, legendary port of Muciri Pattinam".
  - The Sahapedia note (PDF 10) goes furthest: "an important destination on the world map for centuries, as the 5th century Roman map, the 'Tabula Peutingeriana' clearly establishes."
- **Nair, p. 516:** "Pattanam, which could be part of ancient Muziris".

### 5.2 Roman, West Asian and Indian trade

- **West Asian trade before Roman.**
  - PAMA PDF 32: torpedo jars in the transition layers indicate "that the trade with West Asian, South Arabian and Mesopotamian regions had preceded that with the Mediterranean by at least two to three centuries."
  - Cherian 2012, p. 27: "This may suggest commercial activities at Pattanam site in the pre-Roman phase."
  - Both rest on sherds in "transition" layers at a heavily disturbed site (see section 2.3).
- **The Roman peak, quoting Tomber.** Cherian 2011, p. 4: "a peak of Roman contact during the later first century BCE and first century CE, although some sherds continue into at least the second century CE. The only Late Roman (fifth/sixth) transport amphora so far identified at Pattanam, the Aqaba (ancient Aila) amphora also occurred."
- **Who used the amphorae.** PAMA PDF 29: amphorae "are likely to have been for resident merchants and traders from the West, as well as for local elites."
- **Foreigners at Pattanam.** Nair, p. 535: "The discovery of the West Asian ceramics from the early historic archaeological layers at Pattanam have proved the presence of foreigners in the west coast of Kerala." This is a stronger claim than the evidence shown supports.
- **IRW and Buddhist or Jain links.**
  - Cherian 2011, p. 5: "The occurrence of IRW at the lowest layers of occupation is a pointer to the probable Buddhist and Jain commercial links of Pattanam."
  - Cherian 2012, p. 26 repeats the point.
- **Monsoon discovery.** Cherian 2011, Colombo abstract (PDF 15): the Hippalus "'discovery' was a possible construct – to register the flourish of Roman trade."

### 5.3 Local agency

- **Cherian 2012, p. 28:** "Earlier perspectives (colonial) concentrated on the non-local antiquities and ceramics and projected Indian port sites as Roman sites or at the most as Indo-Roman sites! The Pattanam site needs to be understood primarily as an Indian site with Roman or West Asian connections."
- **Cherian 2012, p. 27:** "The pre-occupation with the Romans would have submerged the local and other regional categories."
- **PAMA PDF 8:** Pattanam is "an outstanding example of local settlers, who, with their technology, craftsmanship, architecture and immense natural wealth, made excellent use of both land and sea".
- **PAMA PDF 6:** "The Pattanam finds point to the presiding role of the Indian sub-continent in transforming the Indian Ocean into a trading lake."
- **Cherian 2012, p. 28**, on the canoe and the Periplus: the evidence "corroborates with information from the Periplus that Roman ships anchored off shore and collected spices and other trade items using small watercrafts (lightering)." This sits awkwardly with the canoe's own calibrated range of 1300–100 BCE given on the same page.

## 6. Contradictions between and within the reports

1. **Canoe and wharf dates.** PAMA (PDF 2 and PDF 67) says the canoe and bollards are dated "within the 1st century BCE – 1st century CE". Cherian 2012 (p. 28) gives the canoe "1300 to 100 BCE (i.e.700± 600 cal. BCE with 95.4% probability)" and the bollard "1100 BCE to 1300 CE (i.e 100± 1200 cal. CE ...)". As published in 2012, the canoe's 2-sigma range ends before the 1st c. BCE begins, and the bollard range spans 2,400 years. PAMA's "wharf could also belong to roughly the same period" therefore rests on a date the 2012 paper does not support.
2. **The 'amana' rim.**
   - Date: "5th century CE" on letter style (PAMA PDF 22) versus "at least from the 2nd c CE" with stratigraphy "in agreement" (Cherian 2011, p. 4).
   - Reading: the 4th and 5th letters are "'te' and 'kei'" (PAMA) versus "a" "ma" "na" "tai"(?) (Cherian 2011).
   - Vessel: "large (local) terracotta pot" (PAMA PDF 7), "local pot" (Cherian 2011), "storage jar" (Nair, p. 527).
3. **Iron Age–Early Historic transition.** PAMA PDF 14 gives c. 500–300 BCE. Cherian 2011, p. 2 gives "2nd c BCE to 4th c BCE".
4. **The Early Historic period:**
   - c. 300 BCE–500 CE (PAMA PDF 14);
   - 1st c. BCE–5th c. CE (Cherian 2012, p. [22]);
   - c. 300 B.C.–A.D. 300 (Shajan 2004, p. 313);
   - "16th c CE to 1st c BCE" for the combined core stratum (Cherian 2011, p. 2);
   - "circa. 6th century BCE- 6th century CE" for Iron Age plus Early Historic (Nair, p. 507).
5. **The transition period is "prior to the trade across the Arabian Sea" (PAMA PDF 14),** yet the same spread lists TGP fragments as one of its markers, and PDF 32 uses transition-layer torpedo jars to argue West Asian trade began two to three centuries before the Mediterranean trade. This is an internal inconsistency in PAMA.
6. **Site area and distances.**
   - Area: "about 70 hectares" (PAMA PDF 12) versus "up to 1 square km" (Shajan 2004, pp. 317–318).
   - Coast: c. 3.5 km (Shajan 2004) versus about 4 km (PAMA, Cherian 2012).
   - Periyar: 5 km north (PAMA) versus "about six km to the north" (Cherian 2012).
   - Paravur Todu: a "distributory" (PAMA) versus a "tributary" (Cherian 2012).
   - Coordinates: Shajan 2004 gives 76°10' E, 10°10' N, which is about 4.5 km west of PAMA's 76°12.587' E. This looks like rounding, but it does not match.
7. **2011-season counts: report versus 2012 table.**
   - IRW: 3410 (Cherian 2011, p. 4) versus 2469 (Cherian 2012, Table 2).
   - Torpedo: 1986 versus 1983.
   - Local ceramics: 894,085 versus 894,209.
   - Chinese: 66 versus 64.
   - Amphora (2779), TGP (861) and sigillata (111) agree.
8. **Torpedo jar and TGP totals across years.**
   - Cherian 2012 gives 3098 torpedo and 1527 TGP for 2007–2011.
   - Nair (p. 516, citing Cherian ed. 2015h:25) gives "About 398 Torpedo" and "About 412 TGP" for 2007–2014.
   - A longer period cannot have fewer sherds. One of these sets is wrong; Nair may have taken antiquity-register entries rather than sherd counts. The cause is not stated.
9. **Local body and rim sherds.**
   - Nair (p. 517) gives 300,930 body and 55,935 rim sherds for 2007–2014.
   - The 2011 season alone had 735,114 body and 144,044 rim sherds (Cherian 2011, p. 3), and PAMA gives 4.5 million body sherds overall.
   - Nair's figures are not reconcilable with the primary reports.
10. **Terra sigillata.**
    - 122 to 2011 (Cherian 2011, Cherian 2012) and 171 to 2014 (Nair).
    - PAMA's "Pattanam has produced two stamped Terra Sigillata sherds" and Cherian 2011's single 'VIL' stamped base are compatible.
    - PAMA calls Pattanam "the third archaeological site in India to report Terra Sigillata", which is consistent with Cherian 2011.
    - PAMA says Arretine production ran "from the 1st century BCE to the 2nd century CE", while Tomber (quoted in Cherian 2011, p. 5) places the Pattanam sherds "mostly [in] the late first century BCE and the early first century CE".
11. **IRW origin and stratigraphy.**
    - Origin: "locally produced pottery" (PAMA PDF 52) versus provenance at other sites "identified as from the Bengal region" (Cherian 2012, p. 26, citing Gogte 1997).
    - Floruit: "late 1st c. B.C. through the mid-1st c. A.D." (Shajan 2004, p. 317).
    - Stratigraphy: "at the lowest layers of occupation" (Cherian 2011, p. 5) versus in all layers "except the Iron Age and modern layers" (Cherian 2012, p. 27).
12. **TGP date.**
    - "3rd century BCE and 8th century CE" (PAMA PDF 34).
    - Kennet via Cherian 2012, p. 27: "as early as 5th century BCE to as late as the 14th century CE" at the broadest, most likely "between the 3rd/2nd century BCE and the 7th/8th century CE".
13. **Medieval range misprint.** The PAMA period roundel reads "c. 500 CE – 1500 BCE" (PDF 14), against "Medieval (c. 500 CE – 1500 CE)" in the text on the same spread.
14. **Excavation from 2007 or 2006.** PAMA, Cherian 2011 and Cherian 2012 say excavations began in 2007. The Nambirajan and Cherian abstract (Cherian 2011, PDF 26) says "Since 2006 the site is being subjected to excavations", and KCHR (PAMA PDF 72) says "Since 2006-'07".
15. **The antiquities table's ring-stone total** is wrong internally (see section 4.2).
